# Facts

| id | status | claim | sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| C01_pillars | verified | Webb's image of the Pillars of Creation shows a region where new stars are forming within dense clouds of gas and dust. | https://esawebb.org/news/weic2216/; https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/nasas-webb-takes-star-filled-portrait-of-pillars-of-creation/ |
| C02_cloud | verified | Stars form in cold molecular clouds of 1,000 to 10 million solar masses spanning up to hundreds of light-years; cold gas clumps, clumps grow by collision or accretion, and gravity makes some collapse. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/; https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/stars1.html |
| C03_protostar | verified | As a clump collapses the material heats up and a protostar (a baby star) forms; before fusion its energy comes from the heat of the collapse. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/; https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/stars1.html |
| C04_jets | verified | Young stars shoot out jets; Webb's Rho Ophiuchi image shows huge red bipolar jets that occur when a star first bursts through its natal dust envelope; the region is 390 light-years away (nearest star-forming region). | https://esawebb.org/news/weic2316/; https://esawebb.org/news/weic2216/ |
| C05_ignition | verified | When the protostar's core reaches about 15 million kelvin, hydrogen fuses into helium; fusion releases energy and the star enters the main sequence. | https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/stars1.html; https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/ |
| C06_balance | verified | A star's life is a struggle between gravity pulling inward and pressure from its hot core pushing outward (hydrostatic equilibrium); fusion's energy keeps the star from collapsing. | https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/stars1.html; https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/ |
| C07_mainseq | verified | Stable hydrogen fusion is the main sequence, the longest phase of a star's life; the Sun is roughly midway through it. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/; https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/stars1.html |
| C08_mass_rate | verified | More massive stars must burn fuel faster to keep from collapsing under their own weight; the Sun spends about 10 billion years on the main sequence while some massive stars live only a few million years. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/; https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/stars1.html |
| C09_whitedwarf | verified | Lower-mass stars like the Sun shed their outer layers and end as a white dwarf, a roughly Earth-sized stellar cinder that cools over billions of years. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/; https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/supernovae1.html |
| C10_sn_threshold | verified | Stars of about 8 or more solar masses end their lives as supernovae. | https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/supernovae1.html; https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/types/ |
| C11_bh_threshold | contested | Roughly 20 or more solar masses is usually given as the starting mass for a star whose core becomes a black hole; NASA pages differ (15 vs 20), and Gaia BH3 shows the models are still being revised. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/types/; https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/stars1.html; https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/supernovae1.html |
| C12_supergiant | verified | When core hydrogen runs out the core contracts and heats while the outer layers swell; a high-mass star becomes a red supergiant. | https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/stars1.html; https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/ |
| C13_chain | verified | Massive stars fuse helium to carbon, carbon to neon, and further elements (oxygen, neon, magnesium, silicon) up to iron; each new fuel lasts less time and the final silicon-to-iron stage ends in days. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/; https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/stars1.html |
| C14_iron | verified | Fusing iron requires energy instead of releasing it, so a core of iron can no longer support the star against gravity. | https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/stars1.html; https://chandra.harvard.edu/photo/2017/casa_life/; https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/ |
| C15_collapse | verified | The final core collapse happens in less than a second and the core temperature rises above 100 billion degrees. | https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/supernovae1.html; https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/stars1.html |
| C16_rebound | verified | The collapsing iron core rebounds, sending a shock wave outward through the star: a supernova; the core survives as a neutron star or black hole. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/; https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/types/ |
| C17_outshine | verified | A supernova often briefly outshines an entire galaxy before fading over several weeks or months. | https://esahubble.org/images/opo0412i |
| C18_crab | verified | In 1054 Chinese astronomers recorded a 'guest star' visible in daytime for nearly a month; it was the supernova that produced the Crab Nebula, a six-light-year-wide remnant. | https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/science/explore-the-night-sky/hubble-messier-catalog/messier-1/ |
| C19_casa | verified | Chandra's X-ray image of Cassiopeia A maps silicon, sulfur, calcium and iron ejected by the supernova. | https://chandra.harvard.edu/photo/2017/casa_life/ |
| C20_elements | verified | All the oxygen in the Solar System comes from exploding massive stars, as does about half the calcium and about 40% of the iron; calcium helps form bones. | https://chandra.harvard.edu/photo/2017/casa_life/; https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/ |
| C21_remnant | verified | If the star was near the supernova threshold the core becomes a city-sized superdense neutron star; with around 20+ solar masses it becomes a stellar-mass black hole. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/types/; https://imagine.gsfc.nasa.gov/science/objects/supernovae1.html |
| C22_horizon | verified | A black hole is so dense that at its event horizon nothing, not even light, can escape. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/; https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso1907/ |
| C23_eht | verified | In April 2019 the Event Horizon Telescope revealed the first image of a black hole's shadow, in galaxy M87; that black hole has 6.5 billion solar masses and lies 55 million light-years away. | https://www.eso.org/public/news/eso1907/; https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/types/ |
| C24_binaries | verified | Nearly all stellar-mass black holes observed so far were found paired with stars; in X-ray binaries the black hole pulls gas off the star into a disk hot enough to emit X-rays. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/black-holes/types/ |
| C25_cygx1 | verified | A 2021 study (Miller-Jones et al., Science) found the black hole in the X-ray binary Cygnus X-1 has 21.2 ± 2.2 solar masses. | https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09091; https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Gaia/Sleeping_giant_surprises_Gaia_scientists |
| C26_gaiabh3 | verified | In April 2024 ESA's Gaia data revealed Gaia BH3, a dormant black hole of about 33 solar masses, found from the wobble of an old giant star 1,926 light-years away; the first stellar-origin black hole this big seen in the Milky Way. | https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Gaia/Sleeping_giant_surprises_Gaia_scientists |
| C27_n6946 | contested | N6946-BH1, a ~25 solar-mass red supergiant, had a weak outburst in 2009 and then largely disappeared in optical light; it was proposed as a failed supernova (direct collapse to a black hole), but JWST data show the earlier observations blend at least 3 sources and the interpretation remains debated. | https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01283; https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16121; https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/12621/ |
| C28_enrich | verified | Material cast out by supernovae enriches future molecular clouds and is incorporated into the next generation of stars. | https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/; https://chandra.harvard.edu/xray_sources/supernovas.html |
