Now follow one of the heavy ones. It swells into a red supergiant and starts burning the ashes of its last fire.
Then oxygen. Then silicon. Each new fuel buys less time than the one before. And when silicon fuses into iron, the star hits a dead end.
Fusing iron releases no energy, so nothing pushes back against gravity.
In less than a second, the core collapses. It rebounds, and a shockwave races outward.
That is a supernova. One dying star can outshine its entire galaxy.
We have seen it happen. In the year 1054, Chinese astronomers recorded a guest star visible in the daytime sky.
Today we find the Crab Nebula right there.
