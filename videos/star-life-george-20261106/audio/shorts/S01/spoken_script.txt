It starts in a molecular cloud. Cold gas, with up to 10 million times the mass of the Sun,
stretched across hundreds of light-years.
Some clumps gather more matter, and their gravity grows stronger. One collapses and heats up.
That's a protostar. It isn't burning anything yet.
It keeps gaining mass. Its core gets hotter and denser.
At about 15 million degrees, hydrogen nuclei are squeezed together so hard that they fuse into helium. That's nuclear fusion.
And in that moment, a star is born.
