# Story

**Authorial perspective.** A star's whole life is one tug-of-war between gravity and pressure, and a black hole is simply the round that gravity wins outright — which is why only the rare heavyweights, not stars like the Sun, end that way.

**Architecture: journey (chronological) carried by one mechanism.** We follow a single heavy star from cloud to black hole because the creator asked for "first moment to black hole"; the gravity-vs-pressure balance is the thread that makes each stage a consequence of the last, not a list.

**Title / thumbnail promise.** "From Dust to Black Hole: The Life and Death of a Giant Star" · thumbnail: real Webb pillars on the left, an illustrated black hole on the right, text "DUST → BLACK HOLE". Paid off: the first 20 s name both ends; the black hole arrives at ~5:00 and the journey is complete by the end.

**Core question.** How does a soft cloud of dust become the darkest object in the universe?

**Emotional register.** Wonder (earned by real photographs), a short build of tension at the iron core and collapse (earned by the physics: under a second, over 100 billion degrees), warmth at the elements-in-your-body beat, calm at the close. No manufactured drama.

**Honesty choices.** Real telescope images are labelled "Real image" with the telescope; things nobody can photograph (a collapsing clump, a star's interior, a stellar black hole up close) are labelled "Illustration". The M87 image is shown as a supermassive black hole, explicitly not a stellar one. The black-hole mass threshold and N6946-BH1 are hedged (contested).

**Deliberately left out.** Type Ia (white-dwarf) supernovae, neutron-star detail, gravitational waves, Hawking radiation, the inside of a black hole — each would branch the single journey.
