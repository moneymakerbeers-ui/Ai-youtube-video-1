# Research notes (stage 01)
Core concepts: molecular clouds → gravitational collapse → protostar (heat of contraction) → H fusion at ~15 MK → main sequence (hydrostatic equilibrium) → mass sets lifetime and fate → massive stars: red supergiant, fusion chain to iron → iron fusion costs energy → core collapse (<1 s, >100 billion degrees) → rebound/shock → supernova → neutron star or black hole.
Best examples: Webb Pillars of Creation (star formation), Webb Rho Ophiuchi (jets), Crab Nebula (SN 1054, recorded by Chinese astronomers), Cassiopeia A (Chandra element map), Cygnus X-1 (21.2 ± 2.2 M☉, Miller-Jones 2021), Gaia BH3 (~33 M☉, ESA 2024), M87* EHT shadow (supermassive, used only as "what a black-hole shadow looks like").
Misconceptions addressed: protostars do not glow from fusion; the Sun will not become a black hole; the M87 image is not a star's black hole; black holes are found via companions, not seen directly.
Real uncertainty: black-hole formation threshold (NASA pages give 15 and 20 M☉; Gaia BH3 above model limits) → hedged; N6946-BH1 failed supernova (Adams 2017 vs Beasor 2024 JWST blend of ≥3 sources) → contested and hedged.
What typical videos skip: the honest mass threshold uncertainty; that most stars never make black holes; failed supernovae. (Framing only; no structure or wording copied.)
Left out: Type Ia supernovae, neutron-star physics, gravitational waves, Hawking radiation.
