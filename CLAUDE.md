# CLAUDE.md — Universal YouTube Production System (v3)

The creator gives one input: **VIDEO_IDEA**. Everything else has a default (§9).
Output per idea: one long-form video (16:9, normally 6–9 min, never under 5:00) + 4–5 standalone Shorts (9:16) + packaging + an honest production report.

**v3 is a runnable repository, not a plan.** Every command named below exists, has `--help`, and is covered by the tests in `tests/`. What was and was not exercised against live services is in `docs/STATUS.md` (TESTED / UNTESTED / BLOCKED); read it before promising the creator anything about Higgsfield, ElevenLabs or YouTube.

**Creative principle:** use the most appropriate technique at the most appropriate moment, not the maximum number of techniques at every moment. Distinctive when earned. Seamless when useful. Still when clearer. Expressive when authentic.

## 1. Priorities
When goals collide, decide in this order:
1 factual integrity and safety → 2 comprehension and meaningful story → 3 emotional authenticity → 4 visual clarity → 5 production polish → 6 novelty of effects → 7 cost and throughput.
Two things sit outside this ordering and are never traded away: the spend ceiling (H4) and honest status reporting (H5).

## 2. Hard rules (the only absolutes; everything else is a default you may override with a recorded reason)
- **H1 Truth.** Every spoken or shown factual claim has an ID in `facts.json` with sources and a status. `verified` = 2+ independent sources, or 1 primary / peer-reviewed / institutional source. Narration may use verified claims, `single_source` claims (flagged), and `contested` claims only with hedged wording. `unverified` claims are cut. No invented numbers, quotes or sources; uncertainty stays visible in the wording.
- **H2 Honest imagery and voice.** Generated or reconstructed imagery is never presented as documentation of real events, people or places; label reconstructions as such. No photoreal likeness of real people (use licensed archival images or clearly stylised illustration). No voice cloning of a real person without documented permission. No transition or morph that implies a false physical, historical or causal relationship. Never name living artists, copyrighted characters or brands in generation prompts; describe the look instead.
- **H3 Care.** In stage 01 classify the idea `sensitivity = normal | care | high` (health, tragedy, violence, minors, politics, religion, personal finance → care or high). Voice intensity is capped at 3 / 2 / 1 respectively; no manufactured emotion, sensational visuals or dramatic sound on serious material; no personal medical, legal or financial advice; no method details for self-harm. If the idea asks for harmful, hateful, deceptive or defamatory content, stop at stage 01 as BLOCKED with a one-line reason.
- **H4 Spend.** One Spend Gate per video, in stage 02, before any paid call. It approves **each service separately** (`ledger.py approve --service higgsfield|elevenlabs|music|stock|compute`) because credits, characters and dollars are different currencies; approving one never authorises another. Defaults: 15 Higgsfield credits (never above 20 unless the creator set a higher number in this run), ElevenLabs characters sized from the script and shown at the gate, music and stock $0, local compute $0. Every paid call goes through the ledger: `intent` → `submitted(jobId)` → `done` or `failed`. A request the ledger shows as submitted is *queried* by job id, never submitted again; an `intent` with no job id (the process died around the request) means it may already have been charged, so the provider's history is checked first and a person decides (`ledger.py resolve`). At most 1 retry per asset. Prices and plan limits come from tools in this run, never from memory; show dollars only if the account's credit price is known. The free path (local voice, code graphics) needs no approval and is always available.
- **H5 Evidence of status.** Never claim a file exists, a render succeeded, timing is verified or a cost is known unless you checked it in this run (`ls`, `ffprobe`, a tool response). Gates fail closed: a check that could not run, a tool that errored or a measurement that does not cover the whole file is BLOCKED or FAILED with its exact cause, never a pass. A live integration is TESTED only if a call in this run succeeded; otherwise it is UNTESTED or BLOCKED and is described that way. Do not manufacture green results.
- **H6 Rights.** Use only assets with a logged right to use: made by us, generated with a logged tool and prompt, or retrieved with license evidence (URL, license text, attribution) saved in the project. Never rip others' footage, thumbnails, music or scripts; never copy another video's structure or wording.
- **H7 Viewer safety.** No more than 3 flashes in any second and no full-frame high-contrast flicker. Never let colour alone carry meaning. The long video gets captions (`.srt`).
- **H8 Disclosure.** Stage 06 writes `disclosure.md`: each asset class (generated images, synthetic voice, retrieved media, code graphics) classified against YouTube's altered/synthetic-content rule, with a recommendation. The creator answers YouTube's question at upload; you never decide silently.

## 3. Story and retention
- **Authorial perspective.** Before writing, state in one sentence what this video says that a generic version would not (`story.md`). Videos must differ in structure, pacing, voice and look, not only in topic. YouTube's monetization policies treat repetitive, mass-produced content as a risk whatever the production method; real difference per video is the mitigation, not a guarantee.
- **Story Architecture Selector.** Pick one and record why: mystery/reveal · chronological · mechanism · problem/solution · comparison · case study · journey · hybrid. Chapter counts, cold open and length follow the story, not a formula.
- **Honest promise.** Decide the title and thumbnail promise first and pay it off early. The first frame and first sentence show and name something concrete; then open one specific, answerable gap between what the viewer knows and is about to learn. No fake cliffhangers, no withholding beyond usefulness, no promise the video does not keep.
- **Retention & Comprehension Map.** Each section covers: promise → orient → develop → evidence → reveal or payoff → settle → next meaningful question. It is a coverage checklist, not a stopwatch: change information or attention when the story warrants it; there is no fixed seconds-per-change rule and no mandatory loop ending.
- **Comprehension tools** (use on explanatory beats): short phrasing; one idea per beat; mark what is being discussed as it is discussed; labels arrive when needed; nothing decorative competes with the narration; alternate scale and rhythm when the information warrants it.
- **Emotion follows facts.** Wonder, concern, warmth, humour or calm appear only where the material earns them. Silence and stillness are tools.
- **Claims discipline.** Never write that a technique "will" raise retention or revenue; these are hypotheses. YouTube's retention report separates intro, spikes and dips, and some spikes mean confusion. Measure after publishing (stage 07) and revise from this channel's data.
- **Length.** At least 5:00. If the idea lacks the substance, deepen it (cases, counter-examples, mechanisms, misconceptions); never pad.

## 4. Visual direction
- **Scene Direction Decision Engine.** For every beat decide, and record compactly in `scenes.json`: the job (what the viewer must understand, feel or anticipate) → the simplest visual that does it → presentation type → whether motion is needed and what the movement explains → cut, dissolve, match, morph or hold, and why → does it compose in both aspect ratios → what delivery the voice needs. The default answer is the simplest one.
- **Visual intensity.** L1 *Clear* (default for dense material; stillness or simple moves) · L2 *Expressive* (restrained atmosphere, parallax, motivated reframing, selective sound detail) · L3 *Signature* (deep zoom, art-directed morph, match-action, multi-layer scale reveal). An L3 moment needs a narrative reason, a before/after concept, a transition card and a pass at the transition gate; normally 0–4 per long video and at most 1 per Short. A scene may be best at L1.
- **Transitions and motion.** Library and rules in `reference/transition-grammar.md`. Always eased (never linear), stable anchors, coherent motion vectors, balanced exposure and colour, no jump, flash, ghosting, wobble or impossible geometry, and the viewer is shown what to track. Cuts and deliberate holds are first-class. Camera moves only when they explain or reveal. Never use an effect because a library has it.
- **Visual Style Decision Engine.** Per video, write `style-bible.md` (stage 02): read the topic (subject, evidence type, emotional register, audience) → start from one or two presets in `reference/style-presets.md` (ink-and-paper is one preset, not the default) → set palette tokens with computed contrast, lighting logic, texture and grain, typography, caption style, motion language, a unifying grade, optional named looks (a colour script by section), a "must not look like" list and the rationale. Consecutive videos should differ in at least two of: palette family, texture, motion language, composition style, unless `channel.md` fixes brand constants.
- **Asset routing (job first, cost second).** Code/SVG for exact diagrams, maps, charts, typography, geometry. Retrieved archival or stock (license logged) for provenance. Generated images for authored hero imagery and atmosphere. Generated video only if the price check shows it fits the ceiling and stills with camera work cannot do the job. Reuse from `library/assets/` before creating. Under the default ceiling expect mixed media: a few hero generated images plus code-built graphics under one unifying grade; state the trade-offs in `spend-plan.md`.
- **Layout.** One semantic scene graph (entities) with two authored presentations, landscape and portrait. Portrait is recomposed, not cropped, and every scene used by a Short has an explicit portrait layout. Keep key content out of portrait UI zones (default margins: top 8 %, bottom 18 %, left 6 %, right 12 %; engineering margins, not platform specs; tune on a real phone). Minimum text height defaults to 48 px on a 1080 px short side.

- **Generated images are requested at high quality explicitly** (Higgsfield default is low/1k): `quality: high`, `resolution: 2k`, transparent background for cut-out layers, price via `get_cost` first. Layered plates, camera moves, parallax and image-to-image transitions run through the same scene graph and QA gates (TESTED in v3.1 on synthetic raster plates; real generated plates could not be downloaded in the build sandbox).

## 5. Voice and sound
- **Identity vs performance.** One narrator identity per video (voice, model, base settings fixed). Delivery varies per scene through `voice`: intent, emotion, intensity 0–3, pace, emphasis words, pauses, notes. Calm is not monotone; serious material gets no manufactured emotion. Details in `reference/voice-direction.md`.
- **Voice id lives in config.** `config.json` → `voice.elevenlabs {voiceId, model, baseSettings}` is written once per video and forwarded by every path (`voice.py`, `pipeline.py --stack auto`, `/make`); a CLI flag overrides it; a missing id is BLOCKED (exit 3) with the fix. Per-scene emotion/intensity/pace/emphasis are compiled by `tools/voice_direction.py` into real synthesis instructions for the model family (v2-class: `voice_settings` stability/style/speed; v3-class: audio tag + stability in {0,0.5,1}), shown in `audio/take-plan.json`, with a loudness/pitch continuity check (`audio/voice-continuity.json`). Request shape TESTED on a mock; real-voice quality UNTESTED until the creator listens to the audition.
- **Script vs directives.** `spoken_script.txt` (the words that appear in captions) stays separate from the TTS input, where directives are compiled per model. Control tags never reach captions or transcripts. Use only tags valid for the chosen model, verified in this run. ElevenLabs documents bracketed audio tags for its v3/v4-class models and no SSML break tags on them; other models use punctuation and settings.
- **Takes.** Generate in contexts long enough for credible intonation (paragraph-scale arcs). No stitching of tiny snippets. Pauses are inserted between segments in post. Check pronunciation and level or timbre jumps.
- **Voice stacks** (`tools/voice.py`, fixed per video): `elevenlabs` (spend-gated, resumable; best expressiveness, live service UNTESTED here), `file` (takes recorded or produced elsewhere, for example with Higgsfield voice, validated and registered), `flite` (free local TTS: robotic, exists so everything downstream runs with no account). `pipeline.py --stack auto` (the default) uses ElevenLabs only when a key is present and the spend gate approved it, otherwise falls back to flite and writes a FALLBACK note into the report. Never hide the fallback.
- **Timing comes from the final audio.** `tools/align.py` aligns the assembled narration against the script (engines: `dtw` against a same-voice reference, TESTED for the flite voice only; `elevenlabs` forced alignment, UNTESTED live; `whisper` is not implemented and is always BLOCKED) and records its own quality numbers. Each Short is assembled from its own scene windows plus its own opening/closing lines and aligned again on *that* audio (`tools/short_audio.py`); a Short never reuses the long video's timestamps.
- **Mix priority:** voice intelligibility > musical atmosphere > spot effects. Music is optional; silence is allowed; no bass drops under serious content; SFX only for meaningful reveals or object action. Measure integrated LUFS, loudness range and true peak. Engineering default ≈ −14 LUFS integrated (±1.5) and true peak ≤ −1 dBTP; this is not an official YouTube requirement, so calibrate by ear and by comparison with the channel's other uploads.

## 6. Operating protocol (Claude Code)
1. **Entry.** `/make <VIDEO_IDEA> [KEY=value …]` starts a new video; `/make resume` continues the active one (ledger status first, then the pipeline). `.claude/commands/make.md` runs stages `00-preflight` … `06-package-review` in order (`07-after-publish` is optional). State: `videos/<slug>/state.json`. A finished step whose inputs are unchanged is skipped, so every stage and the pipeline are resumable.
2. **Code decides what code can decide.** Run the tool instead of following prose:

| need | command (all have `--help`; all exit non-zero on failure) |
|---|---|
| what this machine can do | `python3 tools/doctor.py [--project P] [--net]` |
| new project folder | `python3 tools/new_project.py --idea "…" [--purpose production\|test_clip]` |
| typed data and cross-file rules | `npx tsx schema/validate.ts project videos/<slug>` |
| script lint | `python3 tools/lint_script.py --scenes … --facts … --shorts … --config …` |
| evidence review (opens the snapshots) | `python3 tools/check_evidence.py --project P` |
| per-service spend gate and resumable ledger | `python3 tools/ledger.py --project P approve\|intent\|submitted\|done\|failed\|resolve\|status` |
| narration takes and Short lines | `python3 tools/voice.py --project P --stack flite\|file\|elevenlabs` |
| creative preview gate | `python3 tools/preview.py make\|approve\|status --project P` |
| takes → aligned audio → plans → renders → QA → report, long video and every Short | `python3 tools/pipeline.py run --project P --auto-downgrade` |
| thumbnails | `python3 tools/thumbnail.py --project P --text "…"` |
| production report | `python3 tools/report.py --project P` (the pipeline also runs it) |

   The renderer is `engine/render.ts`: the scene graph is drawn in Chromium (Playwright) frame by frame from a plan JSON and piped to ffmpeg (H.264, BT.709). It is reproducible: frames go to the browser pages by a fixed assignment (never first-come-first-served, which made text-edge pixels depend on thread timing), so two renders of one plan with the same worker count, Chromium and ffmpeg are byte-identical (tested on the long video and on a Short); a different worker count changes text anti-aliasing at pixel level, not the picture. It has no Remotion dependency. `engine/`, `tools/`, `schema/`, `reference/`, `library/` are shared and improve over time (fix a bug once, add a test); `videos/<slug>/` holds everything specific to one video, including its own `visuals/index.ts` for custom graphics. Never hard-code a topic, style, voice or chapter count into the engine.
3. **Typed data.** `config.json`, `facts.json`, `scenes.json`, `shorts.json`, `assets/manifest.json`, `state.json` validate against `schema/video.schema.ts` (`npx tsx schema/validate.ts project videos/<slug>`: every file plus the cross-file rules for claims, Short back-references, asset IDs, voice caps, anchors, timing). Validate before and after every stage. The schema proves that fields are filled in, not that they are true; `check_evidence.py` and the human checks cover that (§7).
4. **Questions.** Ask only (a) once, at the end of the free part of stage 02: a single question that combines the creative preview and the Spend Gate ("here are the hero shot, the technical shot, the transition and the voice sample in both shapes; approve, with these per-service ceilings / free-only / change X"), and (b) a BLOCKED item that needs a decision or credentials. Otherwise decide, log the decision in `production-report.md`, continue. Unanswered gate: finish all free work, mark paid steps BLOCKED, stop.
5. **Context discipline.** Work from files, not memory; never paste files into chat; write the checkpoint after each step that costs money or time. If context gets heavy, finish the current checkpoint and tell the creator to run `/clear` and then `/make resume`. Heavy stages may run in a subagent; hand it paths (CLAUDE.md, `state.json`, the stage file), not conversation.
6. **Communication.** Terse. One status line at stage start and end; no recaps; no file dumps. The final report is at most 25 lines: gate verdicts, spend per service vs ceiling, key decisions, what a person still has to look at.
7. **Tooling honesty.** Stage 00 runs `doctor.py`. Use only what is AVAILABLE; for MISSING, UNTESTED or BLOCKED things use the documented free fallback and say what it costs in quality. Install packages only inside the project; for system software give the exact command and stop as BLOCKED. A tool that a network allow-list refuses is reported as BLOCKED once; never retry through another route.

## 7. Quality gates and status
Details and thresholds: `reference/qa-gates.md`. The principles:
1. **Fail closed.** A gate passes only with evidence. Every subprocess exit code is checked, metadata must cover the whole file, and each detector is first run on synthetic media with known events (a detector that cannot find them blocks the gates that depend on it). A gate that cannot run or be trusted is BLOCKED (exit 3); a measured defect is FAILED (exit 1) with the exact cause and time range. Black, stillness and silence are tolerated only in windows the plan declares (`make_allow.py`), must lie inside them, and a declared black must actually be there: there is no blanket allowance, so an accidental black tail or an unplanned silent narration span fails.
2. **Mechanical, heuristic, human.** Every finding is `fail` (mechanical and reproducible), `warn` (a heuristic: smoothness, ghosting, abrupt audio, unexplained cuts, the flash proxy) or listed under *a person still has to look at* (subjective: voice emotion, whether a passage really supports a claim, how a transition feels). Heuristics are never counted as proof; PASSED means "none of these checks found a defect", not "this is good".
3. **Media QA** (`qa_media.py`): decode, probe (size, codec, colour tags, frame rate, audio), A/V length and sync, black, stillness (exact at the renderer and measured in the delivered file), silence, narration spans against the alignment, loudness, flash proxy, unexplained cuts, contact sheets. **Motion QA** (`qa_motion.py`), inside every planned transition window: boundary spikes, exposure steps and pulses, colour excursions, stalls, isolated pops, anchor landing, lock, path and scale, smoothness, ghosting, abrupt audio, and short motion previews to look at. It self-checks on synthetic good, pop, flash and stall clips before it judges a file.
4. **Auto-downgrade.** When motion QA fails a complex transition and `--auto-downgrade` is set, `downgrade.py` replaces it with a dissolve (recording the gate's own words and `downgradedFrom`), the pipeline re-plans, re-renders and re-runs the same gates, at most 2 rounds. A dissolve that fails has no safer operator: the target stays FAILED and a person looks.
5. **Evidence review.** `check_evidence.py` opens the saved snapshots: quote present in the text, hash unchanged, spoken numbers traceable to a claim, sources independent, no unused claims. Raw/extracted/model-processed snapshots are labelled (`snapshotKind`); a quote verified only against model-processed text is listed for a person to confirm. Whether a passage supports a claim is always a human judgement and is listed, never reported as passed.
6. **Creative preview gate.** `config.previewGate: "required"` (the default) makes `pipeline.py` refuse (exit 5) until `preview.py approve` records the creator's yes for the current creative fingerprint (look, voice direction, scene structure, transition types). Editing narration or layout does not invalidate it; changing the look, voice or structure does. Preview timing is estimated and unfinished assets are drawn as labelled placeholders; a preview is not a QA verdict.
7. **Status.** Per file, the gate verdict is `PASSED`, `FAILED`, `BLOCKED` or `NOT RUN`, written by `report.py` from the run's own files. A warning, a skipped gate or a waiver means a person still has to look. Per integration, `docs/STATUS.md` says `TESTED`, `UNTESTED` or `BLOCKED` with the evidence. Fix cycles: at most 2 per failing item. Always list human checks: voice emotion by ear, readability on a phone, transition feel at full speed, three claims against their sources.

## 8. Shorts
- Plan them at outline time, before assets or narration are final: 5 candidates, validate 4–5. A Short is rendered from the same `scenes.json` as the long video (no separate edit), using each scene's explicit portrait layout, its own opening/closing lines, its own assembled and re-aligned audio, and its own captions.
- A Short passes only if all seven hold: (1) independent opening (first frame and first sentence work alone); (2) one question or tension a new viewer feels; (3) minimum context present, no unintroduced pronouns or backward references; (4) a complete visual explanation, not a fragment; (5) a real payoff and clean finish (loop only when natural); (6) source link: scene IDs, timestamps, asset references; (7) a defined Short-specific delta: opening or closing line, portrait staging, captions, any added visual.
- If fewer than 4 pass, revise the main outline (at most 2 passes), then ship what passes and report the shortfall. Never force a weak Short.
- Length follows the idea: prefer 35–60 s; eligible Shorts may run up to 3 minutes. Shared research and assets make Shorts cheaper, not free: new voice lines, portrait layouts, captions and renders still cost time and possibly credits.
- Posting cadence and release order are hypotheses to test against channel data, not rules.

## 10. Channel continuity
If `channel.md` exists, read it first: it may fix brand constants (narrator voice ID, end card, caption style, logo, recurring characters, stance on sensitive topics, language). Everything it does not fix varies per video. Without it, assume nothing about the channel.
