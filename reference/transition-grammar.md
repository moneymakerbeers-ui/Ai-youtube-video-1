# Transition grammar (read in stage 01 for planning, in stage 04 for building)

The operators below are implemented in `engine/src/transitions.ts` as pure functions of eased progress (A starts untouched and B invisible, B ends untouched and A invisible, so a window never pops at its edges) and unit-tested in `tests/engine/transitions.test.ts`. They are checked on real rendered frames by `tools/qa_motion.py` (§6).

Use the most appropriate technique at the most appropriate moment. A cut is a transition. A hold (a scene with `camera: []`) is a decision. A signature transformation is rare and earned.

## 1. Invariants (every transition)
1. **Purpose.** It advances understanding, emotion or continuity; the planned `rationale` says which (15+ characters). If you cannot say what the viewer should track, use a cut or a dissolve.
2. **Eased.** Never linear. Tokens (cubic-bezier, engineering defaults; tune by eye, keep non-linear): `standard` 0.4,0,0.2,1 · `emphasized` 0.65,0,0.35,1 · `soft` 0.37,0,0.63,1. The engine applies the named token to the window's progress. A style bible may re-map the tokens (a storybook look can be `soft` everywhere).
3. **Stable anchors.** The thing the eye follows keeps a smooth, converging path with no velocity reversal and no overshoot unless the style bible calls for a spring.
4. **Coherent vectors.** Motion before, during and after points the same way (or turns on purpose). No contradictory motion between layers.
5. **Balanced exposure and colour.** The mean luma step between consecutive frames stays small; the palette bridges (shared hue or a neutral midpoint) instead of jumping.
6. **No artefacts.** No jump, flash, ghosting (double edges), wobble, tearing, impossible geometry, or text unreadable mid-move.
7. **Truth (H2).** A transition implies a relationship (same shape, same thing at another scale, cause and effect). The relationship must be true or be labelled as an analogy. Never morph historical objects, scientific structures or different real things into each other to suggest identity or cause. Check scale ratios and registration before you animate.
8. **Safety (H7).** At most 3 flashes in any second; no full-frame high-contrast flicker.
9. **Both layouts.** The anchors exist in the landscape and the portrait layout. If the portrait anchors cannot be placed coherently, downgrade the Short's version only.
10. **Budget.** Default: no signature transition. Normal long video: 0–4 visual-level-3 moments, at most 1 per Short. Roughly a quarter or fewer of the boundaries are non-cuts. Zero is a valid number.

## 2. Timeline contract
Scene windows are contiguous. A transition of duration D is centred on its boundary (window = boundary + offsetMs ± D/2); both scenes render during the window. Segment renders never start inside a window. In a Short, a transition survives only between scenes that are neighbours in the long video; otherwise it is a cut. `tools/make_plan.py` writes the windows into `render/plan-<target>.json`; `tools/make_allow.py` derives what the plan authorises; `tools/qa_media.py` and `tools/qa_motion.py` check them.

## 3. Library
| type | what the viewer sees | use when | needs | default D (ms) | implementation | main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `cut` | instant change | new subject, time or place; most boundaries | nothing | 0 | direct | jump cut on the same subject (fix: change angle or scale) |
| `dissolve` | soft cross-fade | passing time, a gentle topic shift | nothing | 500 (300–800) | engine: B cross-fades in over A; no anchors | muddy double image on busy frames (use shorter, or cut) |
| `match_geometry` | shape A becomes shape B in the same screen position and size | a real structural similarity or analogy that explains something | `anchorFrom/To` with same position and scale at the midpoint | 900 (600–1600) | engine: both scenes are moved and scaled about their anchors so the two anchors coincide for the whole window (size ratio clamped to 1/8…8), the pair cross-fades, the rest fades | implies identity where there is only an analogy: say "like" in the line |
| `match_movement` | direction and speed of motion continue across the boundary | following an action, a pan, a fall, a flow | anchors on the moving thing; matching vectors | 900 | engine: A slides out and B slides in along one vector (default horizontal; the plan can pass the anchor's velocity) with a short motion blur; needs no anchor rect | vector mismatch reads as a jerk |
| `nested_scale` | travel in or out between scales | the idea really spans scales (cell to body, street to planet) | a scale hierarchy: each level contains the next at the correct relative size; anchors at both ends | 1800 (1200–2500) | engine: one camera zooms from the whole frame into A's anchor, B is the inside of that anchor at the right relative size and fades in mid-zoom (zoom factor 1/k, k = anchor size ÷ frame size, clamped to 0.05–0.8) | wrong ratios imply false sizes; label quantities; keep ratios honest |
| `occlusion_reveal` | something in the scene passes across the frame and uncovers the next | the occluder exists in the story (a page turn, a hand, a cloud, a vehicle) | `anchorFrom` where the occluding object is | 800 (500–1200) | engine: B appears through a growing, feathered circular clip centred on A's anchor | an arbitrary wipe in disguise |
| `depth_through_object` | the camera moves through an opening into the next scene | an entrance or aperture is part of the idea (door, lens, tunnel, keyhole) | `anchorFrom` on the opening; parallax layers (`depth`) | 1200 (800–1800) | engine: A zooms toward its anchor (up to 3.2×) while B settles from 1.9× to 1×, cross-fading in the middle | the opening's interior does not match the next scene |
| `focus_bridge` | focus leaves one subject and settles on another | attention moves between two things in one space | both subjects share the frame or an obvious link | 700 (500–1000) | engine: anchors locked (ratio clamped to 0.5…2), A blurs out while B comes into focus (blur 1.4 % of frame height) | banding in the blur |
| `truthful_morph` | one outline becomes another | two states of one thing, or one dataset in two forms (a coastline then and now, a curve to bars) | two vector representations; compatible path structure | 1400 (800–2000) | engine: anchors locked (ratio 1/8…8) and ONE deforming outline (`morph.ts`) replaces both anchors; outlines come from the visuals' `path()` | morphing different things; mismatched start points |

Anchored operators (`match_geometry`, `focus_bridge`, `truthful_morph`, `nested_scale`, `occlusion_reveal`, `depth_through_object`) need `anchorFrom` and `anchorTo` rectangles in the scene data (validation fails without them). If an operator cannot run (a missing anchor or outline) the engine falls back to a dissolve and records `fellBack` in the trace; `make_plan.py` warns when the anchors ask for a size ratio beyond the operator's clamp. Decorative presentations (flips, clock wipes, wipes) are not part of the library and are not used.

## 4. Capability gaps and supported fallbacks (do not invent integrations)
| wanted | gap | fallback |
|---|---|---|
| bitmap morph between two photos or illustrations | the engine has no image morph; ffmpeg `minterpolate` produces artefacts | anchor-aligned dissolve through a shared silhouette; or an intermediate plate made with the image model only if it fits the ceiling |
| vector morph between unlike paths | mismatched path structures cannot be interpolated command by command | handled by `morph.ts`: both outlines are resampled to 128 equally spaced points (`getPointAtLength`, or the visual's own sampler), winding is aligned, the points are interpolated; both outlines must still be the same thing in two states |
| depth parallax from one flat image | no depth map | author separate layers (code-made, or separate generated plates; background removal only if the price fits), or 2–3 layer scale/translate parallax |
| unbroken fly-through of photographic scenes | needs generated video or 3D | price the generated video against the ceiling; otherwise `nested_scale` from 2–3 plates with a join hidden in matching texture, and say it is a built illusion |
| real motion blur on everything | render cost | shorter windows, easing; blur only inside the transition window (`focus_bridge`, `match_movement` do this) |
| true 3D camera | needs a 3D asset and a 3D renderer the engine does not have | skip, or pre-render a short 3D clip if the creator supplies one |

## 5. Transition card (`qa/transitions/<sceneId>.md`, for every non-cut and non-dissolve transition)
scene → next scene · type and why now · before / after (and the `signature` track at L3) · what the eye follows · anchors in both layouts · easing, duration, offset and the cue word · exposure and colour bridge · **truth check** (what relationship it implies; is it true; ratios checked) · risks · fallback · gate results · reviewer note.

## 6. The transition gate (stage 04 step 5, and every pipeline run)
(a) **Math, from the spec, in engine tests** (`npm run test:engine`): operators start and end untouched, anchors coincide through the window, size ratios stay inside the clamps, no velocity reversal. (b) **Real frames, `tools/qa_motion.py`** (mechanical `fail`): inside each planned window, spikes at the window edges (`boundary`), exposure steps, pulses and bulges and colour excursions (`exposure`), frames the renderer says moved but the file shows frozen, or an operator stuck mid-move (`stall`), an isolated frame that departs from its neighbours (`pop`), and the anchor's landing, lock, straight-line path, monotone travel, scale jump and aspect (`anchor`); heuristics (`warn`): local jerk (`smooth`), double edges (`ghost`), abrupt audio at the window edges (`audio`). The instrument self-checks on synthetic good, pop, flash and stall clips before it judges the file. (c) **By eye:** `render/previews/<target>/tr-<from>-<to>-strip.png` (10 frames across the window) and the short clip with audio; the tracked element stays continuous; portrait safe zones hold during the move. Heuristic warnings are not proof of quality and a pass is not proof of beauty: the creator judges transitions at full speed.
On a motion `fail`, `pipeline.py --auto-downgrade` runs `tools/downgrade.py`: the complex operator is replaced by a dissolve (`transition.type`, `downgradedFrom`, the gate's own finding and `rationale` are written into the plan, the QA report and the production report) and the same gates re-run, at most 2 rounds; an L3 scene becomes L2. A dissolve that fails has no safer operator: the file stays FAILED. A downgrade is a normal outcome, not a failure of the video, and it is never silent.
