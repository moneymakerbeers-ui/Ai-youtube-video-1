STAGE 01 - RESEARCH, STORY, SCRIPT. Follow CLAUDE.md §3, §5 (script rules) and §8. Video folder: `videos/<slug>/`.
RESUME: read `state.json`; skip finished steps whose output files exist and validate.
NOTHING PAID. Read `config.json`, `input.md`, `channel.md` (if present).

1. READ THE IDEA. Write the interpretation, the intended viewer and one line on why this idea is worth 5+ minutes into `input.md`. Classify `sensitivity` (normal | care | high) with a reason and write it to `config.json`. Harmful, hateful, deceptive or defamatory ideas: stop, mark stage 01 BLOCKED with the reason.
2. RESEARCH (depth per config). Use search and fetch; never rely on memory for facts that can change. Collect: core concepts; the best examples and cases; common misconceptions; real controversies; what a typical viewer already believes; what typical videos on this topic skip or get wrong (note framing only to avoid sameness, never copy structure or wording). Write `research.md` (at most 2 pages of notes plus source list) and `facts.json` (`{claims:[…]}`, schema `Claim`: id, text, sources with type and access date, status, spoken wording). Generate `facts.md` as a readable table. Standard depth = 2+ sources per key claim or one primary/institutional source; deep adds primary literature and a map of expert disagreement. Keep uncertainty in the claim, not out of it.
   EVIDENCE: a source label proves nothing. For every claim the narration will use, save the text you actually read to `research/sources/<id>.txt`, record how you got it in `snapshotKind` (`raw` = the bytes a server sent, `extracted` = text a tool pulled out of them, `model_processed` = a fetch tool that summarises: ask such tools for verbatim passages, and know that a quote verified only against model-processed text is listed for a person to confirm), and add `evidence` pointers to the claim: `sourceIndex`, `locator`, the exact `quote` (copied from the snapshot, at least 12 characters), `snapshot`, `snapshotSha256`, `supports` (direct | partial | context) and `interpretation`. `verified` needs at least one `direct` pointer.
3. STORY (`story.md`, at most 1 page): the authorial perspective (one sentence), the architecture chosen from the selector with a one-line reason, the honest title and thumbnail promise, the core question, the emotional register the facts earn ("none" is allowed), what the video deliberately leaves out.
4. OUTLINE AND MAP. `outline.md` plus `retention-map.md`: one row per section with promise · orient · develop · evidence · payoff · settle · next question, and a "confusion risks" column (jargon, skipped steps). Plan words from the target duration at 150 wpm (re-estimated after voice).
5. OPENING LAB. Write 3 variants of the opening (first 30 s). Choose by this rubric: a concrete subject named in sentence 1; a specific, answerable question; matches the title and thumbnail promise; no backward reference; no hype; every claim true. Log the choice in `story.md`.
6. SHORTS ARCHITECTURE (before narration is final). List 5 candidates from different sections or angles; a Short may combine adjacent moments. For each fill the `ShortPlan` fields (title, one question, minimum context, payoff, source beats from `outline.md` (provisional until step 8 gives them scene IDs), delta, target length) and run the seven checks in CLAUDE.md §8. Fix a failing candidate once by changing its delta or scope. If fewer than 4 validate, revise the outline (at most 2 passes). Write `shorts.json` and a short `shorts-plan.md` table; record any shortfall honestly.
7. NARRATION. Write `spoken_script.txt`: words only, paragraphs by section, no tags, no stage directions. Plain words, short sentences, one idea per beat, a concrete comparison with every number, hedged wording for contested claims, no empty hype ("mind-blowing", "you won't believe"), neutral wording on sensitive material. Write `pronunciation.md`: names and terms with a TTS respelling.
8. SCENES. Split the narration into scenes (one beat each, typically 6–20 s of speech) and write `scenes.json` (schema `ScenesFile`): claim IDs, narration, intent, retention beat, presentation, visual intensity (default 1), transition into the next scene (default cut; anything else needs a rationale and anchors; L3 needs `signature`), voice direction (default neutral-clear, intensity 0; raise only where earned and within the sensitivity cap), entities with abstract `need` descriptions (no file names), rough landscape placements, portrait layout for every scene used by a validated Short, `shortIds`, `qaRisk`. Keep every field short; write rationale only where the choice is non-default. Then finalise the Shorts: replace each provisional beat in `shorts.json` with its final `sceneIds`, set `shortIds` on those scenes, and re-run the seven checks against the real narration (fix a failing Short once, otherwise mark it `rejected` with the reason).
9. LINT. Run `npx tsx schema/validate.ts project videos/<slug>` (every file plus the cross-file rules: claims, Short back-references, voice caps, anchors), then `python3 tools/lint_script.py --scenes … --facts … --shorts … --config …`, then `python3 tools/check_evidence.py --project videos/<slug>` (it opens the snapshots: missing file, changed hash, quote not in the text, a spoken number no claim traces to, sources that are not independent). Fix every FAIL and every error. Then one self-critique pass: name the 3 weakest moments (confusing, slow, manipulative, redundant) and fix them; no further loops.
10. INDEPENDENT REVIEW. Schema-valid is not supported, and passing your own seven Short checks is not standalone. Have a reviewer who did not write them judge, and write the verdicts as data: (i) each narrated claim against its quote and snapshot → `review/evidence-review.json` (`verdicts[{claimId, verdict: supported|partial|unsupported, reason}]`); (ii) each validated Short as a stranger who has seen nothing else → `review/shorts-review.json` (`verdicts[{shortId, verdict: pass|revise|reject, standalone, problems[{quote, problem, fix}]}]`, quoting the exact words). Record `reviewer.kind` truthfully: `independent_agent` (a separate agent that did not write the claims, given only file paths), `human` (the creator), or `same_session` (allowed, never counted as independent: the validator warns, and `--strict` fails). `unsupported` and a failing Short block; `partial` means the narration must not say more than the evidence shows. Verdicts are reasons, not booleans; whether a passage truly supports a claim stays a judgement a person can overrule.
11. LOG decisions in `production-report.md`; set stage 01 to `done`.

OUTPUTS: `research.md` `research/sources/*.txt` `facts.json` `facts.md` `review/evidence-review.json` `review/shorts-review.json` `story.md` `outline.md` `retention-map.md` `shorts.json` `shorts-plan.md` `spoken_script.txt` `pronunciation.md` `scenes.json`.
EXIT CHECKS: all files validate · lint has no FAIL · `check_evidence.py` has no error and its human-needed list is carried into the report · both review files exist with an honest reviewer kind · estimated duration inside the target range and ≥ 5:00 · ≥ 4 Shorts validated or the shortfall is recorded · every narrated claim is verified, single_source (flagged) or contested with hedged wording · the long opening and every validated Short opening pass the independence lint.
